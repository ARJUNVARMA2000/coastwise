Seawall deterioration scoring rubric
Sep 29, 2026 · @Lachlan
How to use this rubric
Each report gets one overall score from 1 (good) to 4 (serious or critical). Higher numbers
mean worse condition.
What is rated: the structure as it stands today compared with its condition when
newly built. Whether it meets current design standards does not affect the score, and
neither do current loads that are lighter than its design loads.
Structures covered: seawalls, bulkheads, revetments, gravity and retaining walls,
groins and jetties, in concrete, steel, timber, masonry, rock or composite.
Basis: the scale condenses the six-level condition assessment rating used byASCE
MOP 130 and the NYC waterfront inspection manual into four levels. Material
thresholds come from the same manual's element damage grades.
Evidence only: score only what the report states. Absence of a finding is not evidence
of good condition unless the report says the element was inspected.
Score definitions
The split between 2 and 3 is whether deterioration is localized or widespread. The split
between 3 and 4 is whether load-bearing capacity of primary elements is significantly
affected.

Seawall deterioration scoring rubric
Page 3 of 5Combining findings into one score
The overall score is driven by the worst primary component, weighted by how widespread
the damage is.
1. Sort components into primary and secondary.
Primary: wall face or sheeting, toe and footing, piles, tiebacks and anchors, armor
layer.
Secondary: cap and coping, railings, paving, fenders, drainage outlets.
2. Grade each component with the material table and the system indicators above.
3. Judge each finding on four factors: how many defects, how severe, whether local or
general, and how structurally important the component is.
4. Apply the extent rules for primary components.
Advanced damage that is localized: score 2.
Advanced damage or overstressing that is widespread: score 3.
Severe damage, breakage, or any finding that significantly reduces load-bearing
capacity: score 4.
5. Limit secondary components. Damage to secondary components alone can raise the
score at most one level above what the primary components indicate.
6. When two scores are equally supported, choose the more severe one and state the
alternative.

Page 4 of 5Edge cases
Missing information: if the report skips an element the rubric depends on, such as no
underwater inspection of the toe, score from what was inspected. List the gap and
mark confidence as low. Do not assume the unseen element is sound or damaged.
Only a rating word, no details: if the report says "fair" or "poor" without describing
defects, map the word with the score table. Mark confidence as medium at best.
Post-event reports: these rate event damage only and may leave out long-term
deterioration. Score what is described and note that the report is post-event.
Repaired structures: score the condition after repair as described. Mention the repair
in the rationale.
Multiple segments in one report: score each segment separately if the report rates
them separately. The report-level score is the worst segment unless that segment is
secondary.
Urgent safety concern: regardless of score, flag any report that recommends closure,
load restrictions or emergency repair.
